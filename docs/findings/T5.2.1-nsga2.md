# T5.2.1 — NSGA-II and constraint handling

**Task:** T5.2.1 (P5, WP5.2). **Status:** done. Implemented in `crates/gwsim-opt/src/nsga.rs` and `search.rs`, and tested on a synthetic problem in `crates/gwsim-opt/tests/search.rs`.

## Source

K. Deb, A. Pratap, S. Agarwal, T. Meyarivan, "A fast and elitist multiobjective genetic algorithm: NSGA-II", *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation* 6(2), 2002. The constraint handling is from its section VI.

## The pieces as implemented

- **Fast non-dominated sorting.** For each candidate, count how many dominate it and list those it dominates. Front 0 is every candidate with a count of 0. Removing a front decrements the counts of those it dominates, and the candidates that reach 0 form the next front. The cost is O(M N²).
- **Crowding distance.** Within a front, per objective, sort the candidates and give each the normalised gap between its neighbours. The two ends of each objective get infinity. For infeasible fronts, the violation counts as one more "objective" in the distance, so their spread is kept too.
- **Selection.** Binary tournaments use the crowded comparison: a lower front wins, then a larger crowding distance. Environmental selection over parents plus offspring keeps the best N by the same order.
- **Constrained domination** (Deb §VI):
  - a feasible candidate dominates an infeasible one;
  - of two infeasible candidates, the one with less violation dominates;
  - of two feasible candidates, ordinary Pareto domination applies on the objectives.
- **Determinism.** Every tie is broken by the candidate's canonical 128-bit hash. With a generation cap, the same seed gives the same frontier (tested).

## Violation and the success threshold

The paper's constraint violation maps onto the success threshold (T5.6.2):

- **per situation** [Proposed default]: violation = Σ over weighted situations of max(0, threshold − win rate);
- **weighted aggregate** (`--aggregate-threshold`): violation = max(0, threshold − weighted mean win rate).

The sum is used rather than the maximum so that a build short in two situations ranks behind one short in one. The point estimate of the win rate is used, not its interval, because the staged evaluation deepens every frontier candidate to 256 runs.

## Operator defaults [Proposed]

| Setting | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population | 64 | §13.3 |
| Crossover probability | 0.9 | Deb's usual value |
| Crossover kind | Slot-level half the time when several slots are free, otherwise bar-level | Slot-level keeps good whole bars together; bar-level mixes skills within one |
| Mutations per child | 1, plus a second with probability 0.3 | Keeps most children close to a parent |
| Mutation weights | Replace skill 0.45, swap elite 0.10, shift attribute points 0.15, change secondary 0.08, change primary 0.02 (human slots only), change rune 0.10, change insignia 0.05, change weapon or headgear attribute 0.05 | The bar matters most; profession changes are disruptive, so they are rare |
| Exploration ε | 0.1 | T5.4.3: one skill draw in ten ignores the slot's role |
| Stagnation | Stop after 8 generations with no new feasible frontier member | §13.3 "stopped improving" |
| Stages | 16 / 64 / 256 runs per situation | §13.4 |

## A deviation from textbook NSGA-II

Evaluation is noisy and staged, so a candidate's score can change as it gains runs. The search therefore re-ranks the combined population after each stage. It also reports the final frontier only from candidates evaluated to full depth (256 runs), so a candidate that was lucky over 16 runs cannot reach the frontier.
